1. Project context
In short: A 126,581 ha upland restoration with peatland carbon at its core and direct at-source relevance to Aberdeen's drinking water supply.
Objective
Landscape-scale restoration of the biodiversity-rich, carbon-rich Upper Dee headwaters, improving water quality at source, sequestering carbon in peat, and delivering biodiversity uplift at a flagship, catchment scale.
Site description and current land use
Upper Dee catchment within the Cairngorms, including tributaries Muick, Geldie and Baddoch. Peat-dominated uplands with low tree cover (~8% in the upper catchment). The River Dee supplies Aberdeen, with a key abstraction point near Invercannie / Cairnton.
Planned interventions
Peatland restoration (top priority), native woodland creation, river and catchment restoration, and habitat enhancement across the estates.
Target habitats and outcomes
Reduced dissolved organic carbon ("brown water") and sediment runoff, improved flow regulation, carbon sequestration in restored peat, and biodiversity uplift.
Constraints and dependencies
Peatland condition data dates from 2015 and is likely outdated, so an updated survey is needed. A quick desktop analysis estimates ~6,217 ha of degraded peat across the estates. Two open strategy questions: is a TEV needed (commission Oliver at NAS), and is the commercial strategy demand-led or supply-led.
Potential questions:
  • Which estates are committed to delivery and over what timeline?
  • Can the 2015 peat condition data be resurveyed to firm up the restorable area?
  • Do we need a TEV before approaching buyers?
2. Supply: which ecosystem services
Interventions map to five candidate markets plus a supply-chain angle. Market context is drawn from the ES rule books (refreshed Oct 2025, from the NatWest Markets Report).
In short:
  • Carbon (peatland) is the anchor: the largest, most active market, matched to the dominant intervention and supported by public co-funding through Peatland ACTION.
  • Water Quality has the clearest single buyer logic in Scottish Water, which abstracts directly from the Dee.
  • Voluntary Biodiversity carries a warm utility and renewables cluster.
  • Replenish and supply-chain resilience open the distillery and bottled-water buyers.
  • NFM is parked pending a downstream flood case.

e. Natural Flood Management (parked)
No buyers identified yet. Headwater flow regulation is a genuine co-benefit, but downstream flood-risk assets have not been mapped. Park until a downstream case is built.

Verdict

Recommendation: Pursue. Lead with peatland carbon at landscape scale, an indicative ~£14M over 20 years on 4,000 ha, against ~6,217 ha of degraded peat identified, with public co-funding available through Peatland ACTION.
  • Water Quality has the single clearest buyer in Scottish Water, which relies directly on the Dee.
  • Voluntary Biodiversity brings a warm utility and renewables cluster (SSEN, Scottish Power, Network Rail).
  • Replenish and supply-chain resilience add distillery and bottled-water buyers, with Diageo strongest because Royal Lochnagar sits on the river. NFM is parked.
Gating decision: whether a TEV is commissioned, and whether GY runs this demand-led or supply-led. The main data gap is the outdated 2015 peat condition survey.

ℹ️About this workflow
Customer Demand Mapping is GY's lead-stage assessment of whether a project is commercially investable. It answers three questions: what the project is trying to achieve, where revenue can come from, and whether the commercial case adds up.
The three chapters
1. Project context, the objective, site, interventions, outcomes and constraints.
2. Supply, which ecosystem-service markets the interventions can sell into, with readiness and sizing drawn from the ES rule books.
3. Demand, the real buyers per market, mapped by hand first and then with tools. It closes on a Verdict: pursue or park.
How it is run
Caitlin owns the run, with George and Katia supporting the demand research. It is manual-first, using tools and the rule books where they save time, and is designed to take an afternoon rather than a week.
How the tabs fit
Content is the deliverable itself, the filled-in template. Workflow is the step-by-step map. To-dos tracks progress through the steps, and Activity shows recent changes.
